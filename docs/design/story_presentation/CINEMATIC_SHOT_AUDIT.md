# Starborn cinematic shot audit

The new-game cold open is the visual reference. Its ten beats use six purpose-built illustrations in 32 seconds. It holds on a frame when the action continues within it, then cuts when the subject changes. The Chime, sealed pod, and Beast are visible subjects. The captions can carry information the image cannot. Keep the cold open as authored.

I reviewed the 12 other illustrated story scenes against their current art, timing, dialogue, and event handoffs. Counts are for distinct image files. A black frame and a new crop of existing art do not count as additional images. Durations are the authored totals before player advancement. This was a shot and source review. Timing and composition proposals still need playback on a phone.

## Production order

1. **One Breath and Across the Tear.** Commission two images for the freeze and its aftermath, then the slipping hands at the Tear. The hands motif can pay off when Gh0st catches Elara in the finale. Match the established crew, Hunter-Killer sprite, tank, fittings, and lighting. In Anchor Chamber, Elara remains physically tethered to the tank.
2. **Beyond the Shield.** Bring the existing coup exchange into the departure. World 3 shows Vale stretching delegated authority. This scene shows that he has taken command from Thorne. Verify the current arrival copy and any references before moving the exchange.
3. **The Final Note and A Long Story.** Add the circuit insert, Elara’s hand, and the saucer. Let the crew’s actions carry the payoff and keep the authored ending state and path to credits.
4. **Airlane to the Spire and the remaining scenes.** Produce the crewed bridge early in this batch so the flight scenes can share it. Prioritize new scenes for events that change a relationship or the player’s understanding. Use the playable Archive reveal as the setup for the coup and consider a short Project Phantom scene once the existing shots are in place.

Review each batch at natural speed and by tapping ahead, using tall Android framing. Check whether the action reads before the caption, whether portrait dialogue covers hands or faces, and whether the scene hands off to the correct room or quest on completion, skip, and reload.
