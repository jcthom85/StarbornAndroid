# Starborn — Master Story Bible

**Status:** Canon Authority — Plot, Campaign Causality, and Ending (Locked July 2026)
**Domain:** This document governs what happens, why it happens, the order of revelations, and the campaign's final political outcome. Lower-ranked documents may elaborate but may not contradict it.
**Tone:** JRPG-leaning Sci-Fi. Humor vs. Cosmic Horror. Found Family.

---

# I. High-Level Premise

Starborn follows **Nova**, a 22-year-old scavenger in a Dominion mining colony, whose illegally modified mining cutter wakes an ancient Echo Relic. By deliberately counter-tuning the machine, she survives a direct handshake and is left **Echo-marked**—able to interact with **the Source**, the psionic frequency of the universe. Nothing in her blood or ancestry makes her compatible. The Dominion Corporation, led by **Director Mara Thorne**, is mining the planet not for ore, but to excavate these artifacts and turn the Source into a monopoly: patents, lock-ins, and a labor world that never stops paying.

Thorne sells the project as “Harmony,” but her motive is ownership: margin, leverage, and a Director’s seat that never has to answer to a board. The Dominion controls facilities built around several Echoes and already builds Source-harnessing tech (Spectral ID locks, relay augmentation, phase-curtain corridor control), but it cannot safely wake, operate, or combine the Echoes. Nova's surviving handshake makes her a mobile interface: the missing link between Thorne's portfolio and root access.

Her fixer, **Lieutenant Arden Vale**, is worse—not greedy, but convinced: a radical idealist who believes the only way to end suffering is to erase the fracture of self.

Nova must assemble a crew of broken outcasts, travel across distinct biomes (Worlds), and stop **Ascension**—Vale’s Soloist reboot of the Great Silence: a reality rewrite that erases personhood.

## IIa. Progressive Campaign Playtime & Scaling Blueprint

To achieve a cohesive **12–15 hour total game length** without making the content feel front-loaded or bloated, the campaign follows a progressive scaling curve. Later worlds expand in physical room scale and mechanical complexity as the party gathers relics and upgrades *The Astra*.

### Campaign Target Matrix

| Seq. | World/Chapter | Target Playtime | Room Target | NPC Density | Relic / Mechanic Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **The Mines** | **1.5 – 2.0 hrs** | 81 rooms (Actual) | High (~8 NPCs) | Onboarding, Hacking, Guard Break |
| **-** | **The Crash** | **0.25 hrs** | Interlude | N/A | Escaping, Splicing the Chime |
| **2** | **The Wilds** | **2.0 – 2.5 hrs** | 91 rooms (Actual) | Low (2 new NPCs) | Camping, Cooking, Tinkering, Erosion |
| **3** | **The Spire** | **2.5 – 3.0 hrs** | ~100–110 rooms | High (~8-10 NPCs) | Lens Relic (Scan), Grid Phase Heist |
| **4** | **The Foundry** | **2.5 – 3.0 hrs** | ~100–110 rooms | Low (0-2 NPCs) | Anvil Relic (Construct), Verticality |
| **5** | **The Void** | **3.0 – 4.0 hrs** | ~120–130 rooms | Medium (~4 NPCs) | Station Infiltration, Relic Integration |
| **6** | **The Source** | **1.5 – 2.0 hrs** | ~40–50 rooms | Zero (Core Party) | Climax, Living Circuit, Final Boss |
| **Total**| **Campaign** | **13.0 – 16.5 hrs**| **~530–590 rooms**| **Balanced Hubs** | **Phase Progression Spine** |

### World-by-World Pacing Strategy

1. **World 1 (Stellarium Colony 4):** Linear corridors and corporate offices. High density of social dialogue and tutorial messaging to ground the player.
2. **World 2 (Sector 9):** Abandoned wilderness and overgrown ruins. Pacing slows down to emphasize survival, camping, and rebuilding *The Astra*. A sparse NPC count emphasizes isolation.
3. **World 3 (Sector 0):** Dense, vertical neon city. Returns to high NPC density (bars, streets, shops) to make the urban heist feel populated and dangerous. Traversal requires scanning phase lines.
4. **World 4 (The Foundry):** Industrial forge built into geo-thermal magma vents. A combat-heavy dungeon crawl focusing on environmental hazards and construct path generation.
5. **World 5 (Orbital Ring):** A massive space station. Highly complex layout requiring the player to merge all previous mechanics to disable sector firewalls.
6. **World 6 (The Source):** A surreal, hyper-focused final ascent. Mostly boss gauntlets, nightmare rescues, and a final choice to surrender root access rather than surrender identity.

# III. Detailed World Outlines

## PROLOGUE: THE SIGNAL (Cold Open)
*   **Setting:** Aethel–Dominion Joint Facility, Sector 9.
*   Clean lab lighting. Fresh steel bolted onto ancient stone. Dominion warning stripes over Architect geometry.
*   Dominion consoles spit graphs and warnings: the Source is spiking.
*   Red strobes. A siren that sounds like it’s chewing through the air.
*   **Lab PA:** "SOURCE BEAST CONTAINMENT BREACH."
*   The far doors bow inward like something is leaning on them from the other side—geometry warping, too big for the room.
*   Orion rips a **Chime** (Ghost Signal Cell) from its cradle: an identity key and beacon carrying his signature.
*   Orion’s voice is a hiss meant for machines and prayers. "Get the signal out. Let Veyra hear me—and let that thing follow."
*   He slams the Chime into the launch tube and hits the release. It rockets away into darkness.
*   For a single impossible beat, the Chorus answers—not a voice, but a soft chord like many notes agreeing to carry it.
*   The shadow at the far doors turns toward the departing signature. The distress signal is also a decoy.
*   Orion dives into stasis. "Mute my signal. Now."
*   The glass seals. A shadow lunges at the pod—then cut to black.

## World 1: The Mines (Stellarium Colony 4)
*   **The Status Quo:** Nova is a 22-year-old indentured "Asset" in debt to the Dominion. She believes usefulness is the closest thing to safety and is desperate to meet quota without owing anyone more than she already does.
*   **Jed's Repair:** Before shift, Jed repairs the cutter's failing **Cryo-Inductor** and patches the **Flux Liner** in Nova's work suit. The inductor can absorb a brief thermal spike; the liner can dump electrical feedback into a proper ground. Neither was designed for Source contact.
*   **The Hook (The Hustle):** Nova removes the cutter's safety governor so its phase oscillator can sweep through frequency bands Dominion blocks as hazardous. She does this to mine faster, not because she knows the bands are Architect technology.
*   **The Wake-Up:** The first uncontrolled surge blows a sector breaker, runs through buried phase conduits, wakes the dormant **Tuning Fork**, and cracks open part of its sealed chamber.
*   **The Punishment:** Foreman **Boggs** catches her. As punishment, he sends her to **Sector 4** (The Dead End) to clear the resulting "magnetic interference." "You like high voltage? Go fix that."
*   **The Twist (Zeke):** The surge flags Nova as **"Asset Liability: High."** The system calculates that she costs more to insure than she produces and orders her **"Recycled."** Zeke intercepts and deletes the order, then leaves his booth when the deletion fails to keep her safe.
*   **The Discovery:** In Sector 4, the Fork's repeating signal is shaking the mine apart. Nova recognizes a feedback problem. Using the cutter's diagnostic controls, the player sweeps a counter-frequency; the Fork answers each adjustment until the two signals come into phase.
*   **The Handshake:** At resonance, the damaged Fork's interface interprets the cutter–suit–operator loop as a calibration rig and opens a neural handshake. The Cryo-Inductor keeps the cutter from melting, and the Flux Liner grounds most of the discharge. The inductor fuses and the liner's sacrificial ground strip burns open; the armor remains wearable, but its Source-ground protection is spent until repaired. Nova survives with acute neurological feedback and an **Echo mark**—an Architect protocol signature burned into her nervous system.
*   **The Pursuit:** The handshake trips Project Harmony sensors. Thorne recognizes a mobile interface, cancels routine disposal, and orders Nova captured alive. The directive carries Vale's calm policy language about preserving "useful variance," seeding him without a full appearance. Local security still treats the order as a lethal containment sweep, making the colony a trap.
*   **The Climax:** Nova flees to Jed. They hijack a rail-launched ore pod, but its authorization is locked. Jed gives her Orion's **Chime**, which Dominion catalogued as a Ghost Signal Cell. It spoofs an old launch identity; it does not fuel the pod. Jed stays behind to seal the blast doors, sacrificing himself by choice so Nova can launch.
*   **The Cost:** The handshake leaves Nova in pain, with temporary sensory distortion and missing seconds. It does not consume or rewrite her memories.

## Interlude: The Crash
*   **The Hook:** The pod clears the rail after the Chime's signature passes an old Orion-era launch check. Zeke splices it into the authorization and navigation bus, not the power core.
*   **The Twist:** The Chime begins seeking its paired Sanctuary and hard-locks the pod toward Sector 9. Zeke cannot override the route and blames himself for making the splice without understanding it.
*   **The Explanation:** The route crosses the outer Planetary Shield outside an authorized corridor. The escape becomes a physics problem, not a fight.
*   **The Climax:** The Planetary Shield flares. The pod slams into the upper canopy and spirals into the Wilds.

## World 2: The Wilds (Sector 9)
*   **The Hook:** Nova and Zeke crash. They are lost.
*   **Recruiting Orion:** The Chime authenticates Orion to the Sanctuary. The Sanctuary's own reserve power releases him from stasis; the Chime supplies no sustained power. He recognizes it as the beacon and decoy he launched **twenty years ago**.
*   **Recovering the Bridge:** The **Bridge Echo** sealed the Sanctuary during the breach, masking the site so completely that Dominion abandoned and quarantined the facility. The party removes the Bridge from its damaged cradle and later installs it aboard the Astra.
*   **Finding The Astra:** The party discovers a crashed Dominion survey ship (The Astra) nearby. It is a wreck, but salvageable.
*   **Recruiting Gh0st:** Gh0st has the party in his sights. Nova's Echo mark passes through relay architecture calibrated from Elara's chordprint and trips his **Source Lock**. The failsafe only interrupts lethal action; it does not recruit him or spread across every target. When the Source Beast attacks during the pause, Gh0st voluntarily protects the party. That is his first decision not supplied by an order.
*   **The Climax:** Using Sanctuary parts and the recovered Bridge, they get *The Astra* running at low altitude. The Bridge connects crew and ship systems without merging their minds.
*   **The Pivot (The Route):** They keep the Astra at low altitude and fly to the Spire along a known surface service route, never approaching the outer Planetary Shield. Internal sector phase curtains block reliable travel beyond the Spire, establishing the need for the Lens.
*   **The Cost:** Nova learns that Source use brings pain, sensory distortion, lost seconds, and mounting neurological instability. Link and Blast burn her out until Orion forces “rest, focus, practice” into the plan.
*   **The Training:** Orion drills a simple ritual—ground first, then channel—so Nova can survive feedback. Later the crew turns that principle into a learned living circuit, not a set of predestined roles.
*   **Breathers:** A mandatory **Canopy Ridge** overlook (open sky + distant Spire vista) and an optional **Tideglass Beach** rest node (food, banter, and a rare moment of calm).

## World 3: The Spire (Sector 0 - The Surface)
*   **The Motivation:** *The Astra* reaches the Spire at low altitude, but internal sector phase curtains make travel beyond known service routes unreliable. The separate outer **Planetary Shield** remains a later barrier to orbit; it was not part of the World 2 flight. They are not going to the Spire to be heroes; they need the **Lens** to navigate the internal curtains.
*   **The Solution:** The **Lens Relic** (held by the Administrator) grants "True Sight" (Scan). It reveals timing windows through internal curtains; once the crew can attempt orbit in a later chapter, the same data can reveal the moving corridor in the outer Shield.
*   **The Heist:** Infiltrate the Penthouse to steal the Lens.
*   **Breathers:** A Lower City **Night Market** detour (optional) and an Upper City **Skypark** (mandatory) to show Dominion’s “utopia” as a costume.
*   **The Climax (The Audit):** Thorne anticipates the theft because she has been tracking Nova as a mobile interface. She is interrupted by a holographic appearance from **Arden Vale**, who frames ungoverned variance as the cause of operational suffering and undermines her authority. Thorne vents the room, forcing a desperate escape. Nova uses the Lens's "True Sight" to find a dangerous exit and reach the ship.
*   **The Cost:** The Lens accepts the Tuning Fork protocol signature far enough to begin a handshake, then exposes Nova to the compliance net. The mark permits contact; it does not grant mastery or obedience.
*   **The Outcome:** The crew uses the Lens to time a passage through an **internal** phase curtain and reach the Foundry. They have not escaped the planet and still cannot reach orbit.

## World 4: The Foundry (Geo-Thermal Plant)
*   **The Goal:** The *Astra* needs a **Deep-Core Engine** to achieve orbital velocity and **Phase-Cutter Arrays** to hold the Lens-revealed Shield corridor open during ascent.
*   **Gh0st's Arc:** This is his birthplace. Project files reveal that Unit 734 was built from a **partial derivative of Elara's neural map**, then developed as a separate mind. Dominion calls him her backup; the label makes him fear that he is only a replacement.
*   **The Quiet (Cooling Springs):** Gh0st recalls Elara interacting with and training him before he had language for their bond. He explains ELARA PROTOCOL to Nova and gives her his override key—trust built through action, not fate.
*   **The Sabotage:** Stealing the Deep-Core Engine physically destabilizes the Foundry. This isn't just scavenging; it's an act of destruction that halts the production of the Ascension Cradle.
*   **The Cost:** Nova’s first attempt to use the Anvil misfires—mastery is earned, and the Foundry punishes hesitation.
*   **Stinger:** After Gh0st’s victory, **Lt. Arden Vale** executes a compliance coup, terminates assets, and relieves Director **Mara Thorne**. He arms the **Soloist** variant because he now states his belief plainly: separate wills create suffering, so one coordinating will must replace them.

## World 5: The Void (Dominion Orbital Ring)
*   **The Hook:** The Lens reveals the outer corridor, the Deep-Core Engine supplies orbital velocity, and the Phase-Cutter Arrays keep the route open long enough for the Astra to ascend. The crew boards the Ring under Vale’s new regime—clean, quiet, and actively hunting them.
*   **Breather (Solarium Deck):** A false paradise route—plants, warm light, a “beach”—before the station drops back into steel and terror. **(Note: This area is locked out after World 5 begins).**
*   **The Goal:** Reach Elara and steal **The Anchor Relic** to stabilize reality, then stop Vale before he completes the Great Silence.
*   **The Reveal:** The crew finds **Thorne hiding in the server farm**. She admits that Harmony was a product-cage and that she let Nova expose sealed Echoes for her. The admission establishes responsibility; it does not redeem her or purchase forgiveness.
*   **Gh0st and Elara:** Elara deliberately calls Gh0st her **brother**. Shared source material does not make him her copy, and her recognition confirms a relationship they formed rather than a role Dominion assigned.
*   **The Climax:** Vale executes the already-deposed Thorne and becomes **The Ascended**, acting on his conviction that his certainty qualifies him to coordinate every mind. He tears open the Tear, and Elara’s signal is pulled into it.
*   **The Cost:** Nova learns the hard way that new powers don’t mean new status—Stasis can slow machines, not an Ascended mind.

## World 6: The Source (Event Horizon)
*   **The Hook:** The party falls into the raw data stream of existence.
*   **The Goal:** Rescue the party from their nightmare loops, then confront **The Ascended** (Vale) at the center of the signal.
*   **The Surrealism:** The Source isn't random; it is composed of **distorted assets** from previous worlds. Zeke's nightmare is an infinite cubicle maze paved with World 1 Mine Access Badges. Gh0st's nightmare is a forest made of rifle barrels.
*   **The Climax:** Vale attempts to overwrite the crew as "broken fragments." Their learned living circuit stabilizes distinct signatures without merging them: Zeke keeps the failing ground path open, Gh0st takes the force meant for the others, and Orion holds the counter-tone while Nova reaches the Key. Their choices demonstrate their arcs; dialogue remains brief.
*   **Orion's Choice:** The Chorus offers a real ancestral continuity, not death or a trick. Orion can join now, but chooses "not yet" and remains embodied with the living people who need him.
*   **The Resolution:** Nova uses the six Echoes to **Tune the World**, collapsing Vale's forced Source network and reopening the planetary phase curtain. She rejects both Vale's single will and the temptation to keep root access for herself.
*   **The Cost:** The tune permanently burns out Nova's Source connection. Her memories and identity remain intact. She leaves with persistent signal tinnitus and an intermittent tremor in her tuning hand.
*   **The Political Outcome:** Vale's Great Silence is stopped; the wider Dominion is not. Thorne's death and Project Harmony's destruction create a regional power vacuum. The crew broadcasts the evidence, and the reopened labor world gains a real chance to organize itself. The colony and nearby sectors may become free, but the future is work, not an instant galactic victory.

## Campaign Causality Locks

*   **Why Dominion needs Nova:** Dominion controls facilities around several Echoes but cannot safely activate or combine them. After the Fork handshake, Thorne treats Nova as a mobile protocol key: capture her, surveil her, and let her expose systems remote suppression probes cannot open.
*   **Why Nova is not chosen:** Another skilled operator with an ungoverned phase tool, a working ground, and the same willingness to answer the Fork could theoretically survive. Nova matters because she causes the wake-up, understands the response as a mechanical problem, chooses to counter-tune it, and builds trust afterward.
*   **How later Echoes work:** They recognize the Fork's protocol signature, not Nova's ancestry. Recognition permits a hazardous handshake only. Every Echo requires a new learned interaction, and none automatically obeys her.
*   **How travel escalates:** The Astra's first flight stays low and reaches the Spire without approaching the outer Shield. The Lens reveals windows through internal curtains and, later, the outer Shield; the Deep-Core Engine supplies orbital velocity; the Phase-Cutter Arrays hold the final corridor open.
*   **How Thorne escalates:** Her policies, branding, and capture orders are present in World 1; her plan to use Nova as a mobile interface becomes visible in World 3. She remains profit-driven and culpable through her World 5 admission.
*   **How Vale escalates:** World 1 seeds his calm preservation policy for "useful variance." In World 3 he condemns ungoverned variance; in World 4 he declares that one coordinating will must replace separate wills; in World 5 he names individual consciousness as the source of pain; in World 6 he enacts the Soloist conclusion. His belief never changes—only his power and candor do.

# V. Character Arcs (High-Level)

*   **Nova:** Solitary usefulness → disciplined trust → leadership → shared survival; gives up root access and her Source connection without giving up herself.
*   **Zeke:** Cog-in-the-machine → defector → crew’s heart → self-worth; learns he doesn’t have to be “useful” to belong.
*   **Orion:** Grief-stricken relic → mentor → truth-bearer → present-tense family; learns the Chorus is reaching back, then chooses to join them later rather than abandon the living now.
*   **Gh0st:** Weapon → interrupted directive → distinct person → brother and protector; learns that being derived from Elara does not make him her replacement. **Phantom Logs:** Throughout the journey, Gh0st decrypts imprints carried by the Chime, recovering fragments of the relationship he and Elara formed.
*   **Thorne:** False savior (PR) → monopoly builder → deposed; admits that Harmony was a product-cage and that she exploited Nova, but receives no absolution before Vale executes her.
*   **Vale:** Polite fixer → usurper (World 4 coup) → Ascended Soloist; tries to author reality as a single voice.

When documents disagree, resolve the conflict in this order. Lower-ranked sources must be revised rather than silently overriding a higher-ranked source.

1. **Starborn Master Story Bible:** campaign causality, antagonist plan, world transitions, and ending.
2. **Lore Bible:** cosmology, technology, Source costs, artifact rules, and terminology.
3. **Character Arcs:** Lies, Truths, relationships, choices, and emotional progression.
4. **Writer's Handbook:** prose, dialogue, UI copy, and tonal execution.
5. **World documents:** implementation elaboration subordinate to the four sources above.
6. **Game assets:** production expression of canon, never the authority used to resolve a contradiction.
